# Glossary

Words and their meanings to help you understand the documentation more easily!

</div>

---

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Regolith** | Martian soil and loose rocky dust covering the planet's surface. | Once Stage 1 has treated it, regolith becomes the mineral layers of the filter. |
| **Perchlorate** $\text{ClO}_4^-$ | Toxic salt found naturally in Martian regolith, 0.4–1 % by mass. | Stage 1 reduces the perchlorate in the regolith before the regolith is used as filter medium. |
| **Bioremediation** | Using living organisms, like bacteria or plants, to clean up contamination. | In Stage 1, bacteria reduce perchlorate to harmless chloride. |
| **Electron donor** | The compound bacteria oxidise to gain energy, here acetate or lactate. | Until a donor from waste is shown to work, acetate is a consumable from Earth. |
| **Chloride** $\text{Cl}^-$ | What perchlorate becomes after full reduction. | One tonne of regolith leaves 1.43–3.56 kg of chloride to rinse out. |
| **Brine** | Water carrying a high load of dissolved salt. | The chloride rinse goes to a brine processor rather than being diluted. |
| **Pyrolysis** | Heating organic material without oxygen to break it down thermally without burning. | Pyrolysis of food and paper waste leaves about a quarter of its dry mass as char. |
| **Biochar** | A stable, carbon-rich charcoal produced through the pyrolysis process. | Biochar is the second layer of the filter column. |
| **Greywater** | Used water from washing and hygiene, without toilet waste. | Six crew produce 33.2 L of hygiene greywater a day at an early base. |
| **Pre-treatment** | A stage that lightens the load on the main treatment, without making water potable. | The column removes suspended solids before the main water processor. |
| **Cartridge** | A sealed, swappable filter module the size of the bench column. | A six-person base runs three to ten cartridges in parallel. |
| **Darcy's law** | Flow through a porous bed is proportional to its hydraulic conductivity and the hydraulic gradient. | Conductivity scales with gravity, so at 0.37 g a bed passes 37.7 % of its Earth flow. |
| **Hydraulic head** | The height of water driving flow through the bed. | Matching Earth flow on Mars by head alone needs 1.22 m of water instead of 0.20 m. |
| **Turbidity** | Cloudiness of water caused by suspended particles. | Turbidity is measured here relative to the influent, never in NTU. |
| **NTU** | Nephelometric Turbidity Unit, the standard turbidity unit, traceable to formazin. | Without formazin, this project does not report NTU. |
| **Michelson contrast** | (light − dark) ÷ (light + dark) of a pattern. | The checker target loses Michelson contrast as the sample gets cloudier. |
| **Influent / effluent** | Water going into the column / coming out of it. | Removal is the drop in relative concentration from influent to effluent. |
| **BVAD** | NASA's Life Support Baseline Values and Assumptions Document. | Greywater, trash and oxygen rates come from BVAD tables. |
| **Reverse osmosis** | High-pressure membrane purification, one option for the main water processor. | Reverse osmosis removes dissolved impurities that the column does not. |
