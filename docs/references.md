## Mars environment

- **Hecht 2009**. Hecht, M. H. et al. Detection of perchlorate and the soluble chemistry of martian soil at the Phoenix lander site. *Science* 325 (5936), 64–67. [doi:10.1126/science.1172466](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1172466)  
  *0.4–0.6 % perchlorate by mass; ice table about 5 cm deep; pH 7.7*
- **Glavin 2013**. Glavin, D. P. et al. Evidence for perchlorates and the origin of chlorinated hydrocarbons detected by SAM at the Rocknest aeolian deposit in Gale Crater. *J. Geophys. Res. Planets* 118 (10), 1955–1973. [doi:10.1002/jgre.20144](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgre.20144)  
  *Perchlorate at Gale Crater, far from the Phoenix site*
- **Davila 2013**. Davila, A. F., Willson, D., Coates, J. D., McKay, C. P. Perchlorate on Mars: a chemical hazard and a resource for humans. *Int. J. Astrobiology* 12 (4), 321–325. [doi:10.1017/S1473550413000189](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1473550413000189)  
  *0.5–1 % perchlorate; hazard to crew; proposes biochemical removal that also yields oxygen*
- **Morgan 2021**. Morgan, G. A. et al. Availability of subsurface water-ice resources in the northern mid-latitudes of Mars. *Nature Astronomy* 5, 230–236. [doi:10.1038/s41550-020-01290-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-020-01290-z)  
  *Shallow ice as the habitat water source*
- **Cannon 2019**. Cannon, K. M. et al. Mars global simulant MGS-1: a Rocknest-based open standard for basaltic martian regolith simulants. *Icarus* 317, 470–478. [doi:10.1016/j.icarus.2018.08.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2018.08.019)  
  *Martian regolith is basaltic, which is why basaltic scoria is the bench analogue*
- **Haberle 2000**. Haberle, R. M., DeVincenzi, D. L. On the stability of liquid water on present day Mars. Mars Global Surveyor Workshop, Boulder. [NTRS 20010084314](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20010084314)  
  *Surface pressure and temperature do not allow stable liquid water*
- **NASA Glenn**. NASA Glenn Research Center. Mars. Beginner's Guide to Aeronautics. [grc.nasa.gov](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/mars/)

- **NASA Science**. NASA Science. Mars: Facts. [science.nasa.gov/mars/facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/)  
  *Surface temperature from about 20 °C down to about −153 °C*
